"Homemade" gets used loosely — plenty of studio-produced content gets tagged that way even though it's shot with the same crew and lighting setup as everything else on a channel. If you're specifically looking for content that's actually homemade, here's what to check.

## Signs a video is genuinely homemade

- **Single camera angle for the whole scene.** Studio content — even "amateur-style" studio content — almost always cuts between at least two angles. A real homemade video doesn't, because there's no second camera operator.
- **Visible real-world detail.** Actual bedroom clutter, inconsistent lighting, a phone or laptop visible in the background — things a set dresser would remove.
- **A consistent, independent creator.** Channels run by the person actually in the videos (not a studio account posting a roster of different performers) are the strongest signal. You can usually tell from the channel's upload pattern: one consistent person/style vs. a rotating cast.
- **Imperfect audio.** Real rooms have real acoustics. Studio-recorded "amateur" content tends to have cleaner, more controlled sound than an actual bedroom recording.
